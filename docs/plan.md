startup, no unnecessary new SaaS, no manual work software can already handle.

# EZ Shots

EZ Shots exists through December primarily to make cash. It is not supposed to become
another big company. It does not need a large team, tons of packages, complicated
software, endless brand work, manual prospecting, or cold calling.

## Pricing

After initial proof:

| Package | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Photos plus drone | $200 |
| Photos plus drone plus video | $300 |

Approximate outsourced editing: photos about $10, video about $25, a photo and video job
about $35.

**Not live yet.** See "What this plan changes in this repo" at the bottom. The site sells
$150 and $250 today, and every price on it is bound to a Stripe link that charges that
exact amount.

## Acquisition

No cold calling. No manual lead sourcing. Software sources agents, filters leads, adds
qualified agents to the queue, and sends outreach.

Target system output while capacity exists: **300 messages per day**, 5 days a week, so
**1,500 messages per week**.

My job is to make sure sourcing works, filtering works, the queue is healthy, messages are
actually being sent, and interested people get a reply. That is it.

## The message

> Hi, this is Angelo. I do real estate photography in the area, and I am reaching out to
> see if we could work together.
>
> If you are not happy with the photos, you do not pay and I will give you $20 for your
> time.
>
> Can I call you tomorrow?

If they want more information, send **ezshots.org**. If they have a listing, move toward
booking. If they are interested but have no listing today, keep them in the follow up
pool.

## Sales process

The ideal funnel is message, interested reply, short conversation, upcoming listing,
booking. Do not add steps to it.

Avoid long discovery calls, Zoom meetings, proposals, complicated presentations, and
manually chasing obviously uninterested people. The objective is interested agent to
booked property.

## Fulfillment

Mine: drive to the property, shoot, upload, send files to editors, quick QC, deliver,
collect.

Outsourced: photo editing, video editing.

Do not personally edit for hours. If fulfillment starts consuming large amounts of non
shooting time, simplify or outsource more.

## Capacity target

Desired eventual pace is **2 to 3 shoots per day**, up to 5 days a week, so **10 to 15
shoots per week** if demand supports it. That is the intended ceiling for a temporary side
hustle, not a requirement to immediately hit 15 jobs.

## Calendar based outbound

Photography outbound is a faucet. Do not create more demand than I can conveniently
fulfill.

| Calendar | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Light | Send about 300 per day |
| Filling | Keep going while capacity remains |
| 10 to 15 shoots booked | Reduce or pause new outbound |
| Future bookings fall | Turn outreach back up |

Simple decision rule: under 5 shoots booked next week, keep 300 per day running. 5 to 9,
keep filling capacity. 10 to 15, reduce or pause depending on the schedule. When the
schedule drops later, turn the machine back on.

## What the workday should feel like

Mental load, expected low to moderate. Most sales activity is asynchronous. No constant
cold calling, no manual list building, no hours of editing. The main mental tasks are
responding to interested people, organizing the calendar, making sure automation works,
and handling the occasional problem.

The physical and logistics load is where the work actually is: driving, carrying
equipment, walking properties, shooting consistently, staying on schedule. The likely
stressors are traffic, a property not being ready, clients changing appointment times,
poor weather, drone conditions, and long distances between properties.

Therefore **geographic scheduling matters**. Cluster shoots into similar areas when
possible.

| Shoots per day | Load | Stress | Gross at ~$250 average ticket |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Very easy, plenty of time for EZ Orders | 2 to 3 out of 10 | ~$250 |
| 2 | Comfortable productive day, about 4 to 6 hours all in | 3 to 4 out of 10 | ~$500 |
| 3 | A real workday, about 6 to 8 hours depending on geography and video | 5 to 6 out of 10 | ~$750 |
| 4 plus | Occasional bonus only | Higher | Higher |

Three geographically close shoots are much easier than three scattered across the metro
area.

Do 4 or more occasionally if it is convenient. Do not design the business around it. At
that point traffic matters more, delays compound, customer communication gets harder, EZ
Orders gets less attention, and the temporary side hustle starts turning into a full time
job. The goal is not maximum possible photography revenue. It is good money with
manageable effort through December.

**The sweet spot** is 2 to 3 shoots per day when booked, and psychologically 2 per day is
probably the easiest money to stress balance: strong cash, manageable driving, less
schedule chaos, EZ Orders still gets attention, less chance photography becomes the main
business. 3 per day is more money and more logistical load, still worthwhile through
December if the bookings exist.

# Daily operating system

## Morning, 15 to 30 minute system check

EZ Orders: outreach running, AI responses working, interested restaurants routed
correctly, signups checked, activations checked, transactions checked, important problem
identified.

EZ Shots: lead sourcing working, filtering working, outreach queue populated, messages
sending, interested replies answered.

If both systems work, move on.

## Daytime

Scheduled shoots, driving, responding to interested leads, booking new shoots, handling
actual customer issues. Do not sit around doing unnecessary admin.

After each shoot: shoot, upload, editor, as fast as practical. Do not let unuploaded jobs
pile up.

On delivery: quick QC, deliver, collect, done. Do not spend another hour on tiny
unnecessary adjustments.

## Time allocation rule

There is no artificial requirement to spend 20 hours a week touching EZ Orders. It gets
exactly the time required to keep acquisition operating, maintain reliability, handle
activation problems, measure performance, and improve the current bottleneck. If that is
5 useful hours one week, fine. If a technical problem needs 20 hours, do 20. Do not
optimize for hours. Optimize for outcomes.

## Work priority order

1. EZ Orders critical reliability issue
2. Paying or scheduled photography appointment
3. Interested EZ Orders prospect
4. Interested EZ Shots prospect
5. EZ Orders acquisition or activation bottleneck
6. Upload and delivery of paid photography work
7. Metrics and system monitoring
8. Everything else

No random projects.

## Weekly EZ Orders scorecard

Outreach sent, positive replies, signups, new active restaurants, total active
restaurants, never activated, churn, orders per account, revenue per account, direct

transportation, transfers. Do not mentally spend the displayed balance.

**Cash App** is the casual consumption limit. Start at $100 per week, normal maximum $100

returns to $0, no casual food, no random purchases without existing cash, no treating

Weekly money routine: check required bills, reserve taxes, protect the current savings

Investing buckets, in order: taxes, business reserve, emergency fund, car and move cash,

# What this plan changes in this repo

This site is the EZ Shots funnel. Five things in the plan land on it, and four of them are
not code.

1. **The $200 and $300 pricing is not live and must not be shipped early.** The site sells
   Listing Essentials $150 and Listing Pro $250, half price on a first shoot at $75 and
   $125, and each of those four numbers is bound to a Stripe link that charges that exact
   amount. See the link table in `PROJECT-STATE.md`. Changing the copy without new links
   recreates the worst bug this site can have, a button whose number does not match its
   checkout. Two new Stripe links at $200 and $300 have to exist first, and the plan says
   the change happens "after initial proof" anyway.
2. **The weekly money model assumes that pricing.** A $250 blended average ticket needs
   $200 and $300 in a rough 50/50 mix. On today's prices a 50/50 mix of repeat clients
   blends to about $200, and first shoots at $75 and $125 blend to about $100. So 10
   shoots a week is roughly $2,000 gross at today's prices, not $2,500, and less than that
   while most agents are still on their first shoot.
3. **The outreach message sends people to ezshots.org, and no domain is connected yet.**
   `docs/site.md` still lists the public domain as outstanding and nothing in the repo
   references the name. That domain has to resolve to this site before 300 messages a day
   start pointing at it.
4. **The message and the site agree on the offer**, which is worth keeping that way. "You
   do not pay and I will give you $20 for your time" is the same promise as `guarantee.html`
   and `refund.html`. If one is reworded, reword all three. The message does not mention
   the 50% first shoot discount, which is fine, that lands on the page.
5. **Volume turns two known blockers into prerequisites.** The checkout still presents as
   Design Byte Agency selling "Photography Pictures NO VIDEO", and the EmailJS template ID
   is still unconfirmed, which loses leads silently while reporting success. At 5 leads a
   week those are bugs. At 1,500 messages a week they are the whole funnel. Both are in
   "Blocked on a human" in `PROJECT-STATE.md`.
