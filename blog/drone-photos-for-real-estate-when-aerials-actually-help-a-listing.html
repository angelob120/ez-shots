Drone Photos for Real Estate: When Aerials Actually Help a Listing

I shot a house last week in Rochester Hills. Four acres, a pool, a barn, the whole property stretched out behind the colonial. The listing agent almost skipped the drone. She told me later that seeing the aerial made a buyer who lived in a condo in Troy drive 45 minutes to see it. The lot alone sold the drive.

What aerials actually show that ground shots cannot

The best drone photos do something no ground-level image can. They show the shape of the land, the relationship between the house and its surroundings, and features that would never fit in a normal frame. A pond you did not know was there. How the backyard backs up to woods. The long driveway that makes the property feel more private than the street view suggests.

  • Properties over one acre almost always benefit
  • Waterfront or pond-front lots show the full shoreline
  • Homes with pools, tennis courts, or outbuildings need aerials to put them in context
  • Neighborhoods with walkable common areas or trails nearby get a boost from showing the connection

When ground-level photos do the job just fine

If you are listing a house on a standard quarter-acre lot in a subdivision, the aerial is not going to reveal anything the buyer does not already expect. The neighbors are close, the yard is average, and from above it looks like every other lot on the street. Spending money on drone aerials here does not hurt, but it does not move the needle either.

  • Typical suburban lots under half an acre
  • Condos and townhomes where the building covers most of the lot
  • Urban homes with zero setback or minimal outdoor space
  • Heavily wooded lots where the canopy blocks everything

One specific thing to do this week

Before your next listing appointment, walk the property and ask yourself one question: is there something about this land that a buyer would need to see from above to appreciate? If the answer is yes, make drone photos part of the package. If the answer is no, save the cost and spend your photography budget on more interior angles that actually matter.

Most listing agents in Oakland and Macomb counties include drone aerials automatically because they know some listings need them and it is simpler than explaining the difference every time. The photographer handles it. You just need to know when to ask for extra emphasis on the aerials versus when to let them sit quietly in the gallery as a nice-to-have.
